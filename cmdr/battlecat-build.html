1Summary

The BattleCat tile in cmdr/datacenter.html already specifies a complete operational behaviour: three cats in three kennels on the generator bus, auto-dispatch on a SONAR alert, a ring-road sortie, arm-to-diagnostic-port docking, a five-item checklist, and a pushed assessment carrying a verdict, a confidence figure, an original-versus-revised ETR and a recommendation. This report turns that behaviour into an engineering specification for a physical prototype: what the vehicle must be able to do, what it must be built from, how it docks without ever touching a live conductor, how it is powered when the site is dark, how it talks to SONAR-mobile, and how it stays inside the safety envelope of an energized electrical campus.

The design decision that shapes everything else is read-only, port-mediated diagnosis. The cat never opens a door, never operates a breaker and never enters a shock-protection boundary. It reaches a standard faceplate (the BC-PORT) on the outside of the asset's control enclosure, reads the controller over the asset's own service protocol, and confirms what it reads with a thermal image, an HMI photograph and an acoustic sweep. Everything it produces is evidence for a person; the tile's rec field is a recommendation, not a command.

Why this is worth building. Uptime Institute's 2026 outage analysis again finds power the leading cause of impactful outages, with UPS, transfer-switch and generator failures dominant, and notes operators shifting investment toward automation and control systems. The three demo outages in the tile (utility feed lost, UPS on battery with a slow generator, a cooling-loop VFD fault) are exactly that failure set. Robots already walk data halls on scheduled rounds — Digital Realty's ANYmal in Zurich runs nine missions a day over roughly 70 monitored elements; Novva runs Spot fleets in Utah. What none of them do today is respond to an alert, dock to the asset and return a decision. That gap is BattleCat.

2What the tile commits to

The BATTLECAT module (about 95 lines of the phone edition) is the behavioural contract. A physical prototype has to honour it, because the phone, the Guide lessons and the desktop console already depend on it.

Fleet
Three cats BC-1, BC-2, BC-3, one per kennel, each with a battery percentage and a sortie count. A cat is only eligible when state === 'ready' and battery ≥ 25 %.
Kennels
K-N Kennel North (ring top, x 162–182), K-E Kennel East (ring right, y 212–232), K-S Kennel South (ring bottom, x 290–310). Each has a dock point on the ring road and a status LED. The note under the fleet list reads "on the generator bus with its own 6 h battery, so cats recharge when the site is dark."
Route
A rectangular ring road from (20,20) to (500,400) in scene units; the campus scene is 520 × 420 units, so a unit is on the order of a metre. Path length ≈ 1,720 units. The cat picks the shorter direction around the ring and leaves the road only for the final approach to the target.
Dispatch
Nearest ready cat by ring-road distance. Only one sortie at a time (the module guards with if(sortie)). A live SONAR alert with auto on dispatches to the generic Hall 2 target 900 ms after the alert lands.
Sortie phases
out → dock → scan → back, then charging until 100 %, then ready. Docking is announced as "arm on diagnostic port". Every completed check costs 2 % battery; driving costs 0.03 % per animation step.
Targets
u1 Service entrance · Feed A (z 0), u2 UPS-2B · Hall 2 (z 0), u3 CRAH-14 · Hall 1 roof (z 44), plus generic hall / yard / road targets w0–w3.
Checklists
Five checks per target class, each a triple [name, reading, status] with status on / warn / bad. The generic list is panel indication read via HMI camera, thermal sweep, breaker states, local alarms and access route.
Verdict
{v, c, etr:[original, revised], rec} — a sentence, a confidence percentage (80–92 in the demo), the ETR pair and a recommendation. Pushed as BC_ASSESS with the check triples attached, echoed as a system MSG on site:<site>, kept in a 20-deep history.
Events
BC_EVENT {cat, ev:'dispatch', target, site} on dispatch. Recall sends the cat straight back. Console audits both message types.

Reading the tile as requirements

Three things in the tile are aspirational for a first prototype and are called out as such rather than quietly dropped. Roof access: target u3 is on the Hall 1 roof (z 44). A ground vehicle needs a rooftop ramp or an elevator; Rev A specifies an elevator-capable cat and a fallback ground-level CRAH port in the mechanical gallery, and treats true roof-top work as a Rev B tethered-drone payload. Single-sortie fleet: the tile allows one sortie at a time; the hardware supports three concurrent sorties, and the tile guard becomes a policy switch. Zero-metre ring road: scene units are treated as metres for this report (ring ≈ 480 × 380 m, perimeter ≈ 1.7 km), which sets the drive-time budget in §3.

3Mission and CONOPS

A sortie is one alert, one asset, one verdict. The cat is not a patrol robot between alerts; it is a first responder that arrives before people can and returns evidence people can act on. Scheduled rounds (thermal baselines, gauge reads) are a secondary mission that keeps baselines fresh for the confidence model in §9.

Operating modes

  • Respond — auto-dispatch on a SONAR alert (the tile's default). One cat per alert; the console can retarget.
  • Deploy — an operator taps a listed outage on the phone or console; identical to Respond but the target is explicit.
  • Hold — remain docked on a P1 asset, re-reading on a cadence until acknowledged.
  • Rounds — scheduled thermal/acoustic baselines of the gen yard, switchgear and UPS rooms between alerts, so the confidence model has a "normal" to compare against. This is the mission the industry already runs with quadrupeds and is the lowest-risk way to log hours on the platform.
  • Drill — the phone's Drill Builder releases EXERCISE alerts; the cat sorties for real but every message is tagged test:true and the verdict is suffixed EXERCISE.

4Capabilities required to run the assessment

Ten capabilities, each with a threshold and the tile check it enables. A prototype that cannot meet a threshold should say so in its BC_ASSESS rather than guess; the confidence figure in §9 is where that honesty is scored.

C1 · MOBILITY
Drive the ring road and the halls
Outdoor asphalt and concrete with 8 % ramps, kerb cuts, wet surfaces; indoor raised floor and epoxy. 1.5 m/s cruise outdoors, 0.8 m/s indoors, 0.3 m/s within 3 m of a person. Doors: card-reader-actuated automatic doors on the cat's route; no door manipulation in Rev A.
Enables: reaching every target in ≤ 4 min; "Access route · clear".
C2 · LOCALISATION
Know where it is on the campus map without beacons
3D-lidar SLAM against a surveyed campus map, ±10 cm on the ring road, ±3 cm at the port approach using the port fiducial. GNSS is a sanity check outdoors only; the halls have none. Works with lights off (lidar is active; cameras carry their own illumination).
Enables: the tile's x/y cat position on the ISO map; docking search start pose.
C3 · PERCEPTION
See heat, read displays, hear discharge
Radiometric LWIR 640 × 512, ±2 °C or ±2 %, 2–20 m stand-off; 4K visible with 20× optical zoom and a 3,000+ lm spotlight to read HMI screens, breaker flags and gauges; an acoustic array covering 2–100 kHz for partial discharge, corona, and a failed fan's bearing note.
Enables: "Thermal · xfmr A bushings", "Panel indication · read via HMI camera", "CRAH-14 fan · stopped".
C4 · MANIPULATION
Mate a probe to the BC-PORT
6-axis arm, ≥ 0.9 m reach, 3 kg tool, 6-axis force/torque sensing with a compliant wrist; fiducial-guided approach then force-controlled spiral search and insertion under 15 N. Port height 0.9–1.2 m above floor. Mate/unmate cycle ≤ 60 s. Retract on any fault.
Enables: the whole "docked · arm on diagnostic port" phase.
C5 · DATA ACQUISITION
Read the controller in its own language
Modbus RTU over RS-485 and Modbus TCP, SNMP (RFC 1628 UPS MIB), BACnet/IP and MS/TP, and a DNP3/IEC 61850 MMS client for switchgear IEDs. Read-only: only FC 01–04 on Modbus, GET on SNMP, ReadProperty on BACnet. Per-asset register maps loaded from the site's asset library.
Enables: "Feed A breaker 52-A", "ATS-1 position", "String voltage", "G3 starter battery", "VFD fault F07".
C6 · REASONING
Turn readings into a verdict with a confidence
A checklist engine executes the target class's checks, tags each on / warn / bad against thresholds and baselines, and a rules-plus-model scorer produces the verdict sentence, the revised ETR and the recommendation. Runs on the cat; no cloud dependency during an outage.
Enables: v, c, etr, rec.
C7 · COMMS
Stay connected when the site is dark
Wi-Fi 6 on the OT network as primary, private LTE/5G as secondary, and a kennel-relayed LoRa/900 MHz telemetry channel that carries the compact BC_ASSESS even when both are down. Store-and-forward for full evidence (images, audio).
Enables: the push to phone, site channel and console.
C8 · ENERGY
Charge from a kennel that has power when nothing else does
Kennel on the generator bus through its own ATS, with a 6 h local battery. Wireless charging pad, ≥ 300 W nominal, 1 kW option, so the cat docks without contacts to corrode. Cat pack sized for two sorties plus 30 % reserve.
Enables: "Kennels on the gen bus · 6 h battery each".
C9 · SAFETY
Share the road and respect the boundaries
PLd safety functions per ISO 13849 (personnel detection, speed limiting, e-stop); ANSI/A3 R15.08-1 and ISO 3691-4 conformance; geo-fenced exclusion inside NFPA 70E limited-approach boundaries for every exposed energized part on the site's arc-flash study.
Enables: "never the live bus" as a hard, testable rule.
C10 · CYBER
Be a reader, never a path
Diagnostic traffic on an isolated, read-only diagnostics VLAN presented at the BC-PORT through a one-way gateway; no write function codes in firmware; signed firmware; the cat's own radios never bridge to the OT network. Every read is logged with asset id, register and timestamp.
Enables: an ATO/OT-security story a data-center operator can accept.

5Vehicle design and attributes

The tile draws the cat as a wheeled body with two eyes and an arm, and the mission is a road with a short indoor tail. A wheeled skid-steer chassis is the right prototype answer: it is fast on the ring road, carries a heavier arm and battery than a quadruped, is cheaper, and the campus has ramps rather than stairs. Legged platforms remain the reference for stair-bound halls and are noted in §13.

6Docking and the diagnostic port

Docking is where the agreed framing — never the live bus — becomes hardware. The cat does not open any enclosure. Each assessable asset gets a BC-PORT: a keyed, shuttered faceplate on the outside of the control section of the enclosure, wired only to the controller's service interface through a read-only gateway. The faceplate carries a fiducial that tells the cat which asset it is and where the socket is to a few millimetres.

Port placement rules

  • On the control section of the enclosure (Class 2 / SELV wiring only), never on a power section door.
  • Outside the NFPA 70E limited approach boundary of any exposed energized part when doors are closed — which is the normal state; the cat never mates to an enclosure with a door open (the port LED reads lockout from the door switch).
  • 0.9–1.2 m above finished floor, ≥ 1.2 m clear floor in front, no cable trays below knee height on the approach.
  • One port per assessable asset; the site library maps port id → asset → register map → checklist.
  • For the 34.5 kV service entrance the port goes on the low-voltage relay / ATS control cabinet; the medium-voltage gear is assessed by thermal and acoustic from ≥ 2 m only.

Retrofit cost per asset is dominated by the gateway and the electrician's hour, not the plate. A site of 40 assessable assets is a one-week retrofit alongside a normal maintenance window.

7Kennel and energy

The kennel is the reason the fleet still works when the site is on generator, on battery, or dark. Each kennel is a weatherproof shelter with a roll-up door on the ring road, a wireless charge pad, a small ATS, a 6 h battery and a gateway radio. It is fed from the generator-backed distribution, so it is live within seconds of an outage, and bridges the gap if the generators fail to start — the exact scenario in demo outage u2.

Placement and coverage on the sample campus

The tile places the kennels at the top, right and bottom of the ring. Treating scene units as metres, the ring is roughly 480 × 380 m and the three kennels sit at the mid-top, mid-right and mid-bottom, so the worst-case road distance from the nearest kennel to any point on the ring is about 400 m — under five minutes at 1.5 m/s. Adding a fourth kennel at the mid-left (by the service entrance and Hall 1) brings the worst case under 300 m and puts a cat beside the highest-consequence asset. Rev A keeps three to match the tile; the fourth is a recommendation.

9Assessment logic and confidence

The tile returns a verdict, a confidence and a revised ETR for each target. On the prototype these come from a deterministic checklist engine plus a scoring layer, so that every number in a BC_ASSESS can be traced to the readings that produced it.

Fault trees for the three demo classes

  • Feed loss (u1): feed dead and other feed nominal and 52-A open with lockout and no trip target and bushings at baseline ⇒ "upstream on the utility side, no on-site damage, safe to hold on generator" — the tile's verdict at 92. Any trip target or bushing hot-spot flips to "on-site fault, do not attempt re-close, dispatch electrician" with a longer ETR.
  • UPS on battery (u2): static switch on battery and input absent and a genset with crank-fail ⇒ the root cause is the generator, not the UPS; recommend paralleling a spare unit and swapping the starter battery. If the input is present and the UPS is still on battery, the verdict becomes a UPS fault and the recommendation is bypass-to-maintenance.
  • CRAH down (u3): VFD fault code present and chilled water nominal ⇒ VFD, not the loop; manual reset restores the unit. If chilled-water supply is off-band the verdict escalates to the plant.

Each rule is a readable file in the site library; adding an asset class is adding a checklist, a fault tree and a register map, not retraining anything. A learned anomaly model over the rounds baselines is a Rev B addition that can only raise a warn, never clear one.

10SONAR integration and wire schema

The prototype speaks the same two message types the tile publishes today, over the same site channel, so the phone, the desktop console's audit and the Guide's live copy work unchanged. A fleet controller in the kennel gateway (or, later, in the console) replaces the JavaScript sortie loop; the tile's BATTLECAT module becomes a renderer of real state instead of a simulator.

Wire schema — as the tile publishes it today

// BC_EVENT · on dispatch (cmdr/datacenter.html)
{ "cat": "BC-2", "ev": "dispatch", "target": "UPS-2B · battery discharge", "site": "br-ldn1" }

// BC_ASSESS · on completion
{ "ts": 1758400000000, "cat": "BC-2", "target": "UPS-2B · battery discharge", "tid": "u2",
  "checks": [["String voltage","412 V · 11 min at load","warn"], …],
  "v": "UPS-2B is holding · G3 starter battery is the root cause, not the UPS",
  "c": 88, "etr": ["10 min","6 min"], "rec": "Parallel G5 to the bus now · swap G3 starter battery · retest crank" }

Proposed additions (backwards-compatible, all optional)

// BC_EVENT · ev is now an enum; extra fields describe the physical sortie
ev: "dispatch" | "enroute" | "arrived" | "docked" | "check" | "assessed" | "unmated" | "returning" | "kennel" | "fault" | "recall" | "kennel_state"
pos: [x, y, z]          // scene units, feeds the ISO map directly
batt: 0–100             // replaces the simulated drain
port: "UPS-2B"          // BC-PORT id; ev:"docked" carries mate force and time-to-mate
kennel: { id:"K-E", src:"gen"|"utility"|"battery", soc: 0–100, door:"open"|"closed" }

// BC_ASSESS · evidence and provenance
evidence: [{ check: 0, src: "modbus:pcc:40012", raw: 10.9, unit: "V", corr: "acoustic:no-engine-note" }, …]
attach: [{ kind: "thermal"|"photo"|"acoustic", ref: "bc://BC-2/2026-09-20T08-05/thermal-01.png" }]  // store-and-forward
caps: { primary_missing: false, map_version: "pcc-3.3-2024.2" }   // why confidence is what it is
test: true                // from an EXERCISE alert
hold: { every: 60, until: "ack" }  // P1 hold-on-station cadence

The console today aliases unknown site ids to br-ldn1 and audits both types; nothing above changes that. The phone's tile needs one change to consume real state: replace the internal step() loop with a bus listener that applies pos, batt and ev — a small patch script in the existing tools/ pattern.

11Safety and compliance

The vehicle is a mobile manipulator working near people and energized equipment. Two bodies of rules apply: mobile-robot safety (people and vehicles on the road) and electrical safety (where the cat and the arm may go). The design treats the electrical rules as geometry: boundaries from the site's arc-flash study are loaded into the map as keep-out zones the planner cannot enter and the arm cannot reach into.

Test cards that map to the tile's QA

The phone edition already has a Playwright QA discipline; the prototype adds physical test cards with the same names so a drill result reads the same on the bench and in the field.

  • bc.dispatch.nearest — three cats ready, alert at each target; the cat with the shortest ring-road distance rolls; log the choice.
  • bc.transit.budget — 20 sorties per target from each kennel, day and night, wet and dry; 90th percentile ≤ 4 min.
  • bc.dock.first_time — 100 mates per port from the autonomy approach pose; ≥ 98 % first-attempt, 100 % within one retry, peak force ≤ 15 N.
  • bc.readonly.assert — inject a write function code from the head; gateway drops it, head logs a fault, assessment continues with that check marked missing.
  • bc.boundary.keepout — place an energized-part keep-out polygon across the approach; planner refuses; arm refuses; BC_EVENT ev:"fault" published.
  • bc.kennel.dark — pull the kennel feeder with a cat at 30 %; kennel switches to battery within 20 ms; cat reaches 100 % within the 6 h window.
  • bc.comms.degrade — kill Wi-Fi, then LTE; assessment still lands on the phone via LoRa with attachments following when a link returns.
  • bc.sonar.roundtrip — Drill Builder EXERCISE alert → BC_ASSESS with test:true on the phone, site channel and console; console audit row present; recall from the phone returns the cat.
